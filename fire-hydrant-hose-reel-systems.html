Hydrants and hose reels

The last line of defence is the one that has to work when nobody is there

The scenario. An external hydrant and a handful of hose reels are fitted so the building meets its compliance requirement. The water is there. The question is whether anyone on site would know how to reach the right landing valve, and whether anything was ever done to prove the flow.

Hydrant and hose reel systems are passive: they depend on a person, a procedure and available pressure. That makes the weak points organisational rather than mechanical. Outlet signage, clear pipe runs, accessible landing valves and a documented flow test matter more than the fitting itself, because a system nobody can locate under pressure provides no protection at all.

Typical impact. Clear signage, an accessible landing valve and a recorded flow test are what turn an installed system into a usable one. A blocked or unmarked outlet costs exactly the time the system was meant to buy.

  • Failure mode: Outlets that cannot be found or reached under pressure
  • Primary levers: Signage, accessibility, recorded flow tests
  • Also required: Pressure maintenance and valve servicing
  • Service interval: Six-monthly valve checks, annual flow test
