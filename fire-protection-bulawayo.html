General industry scenario — illustrative, not an account of a specific client site.

Whole-site protection

Most buildings are under-protected at the edges, not the centre

The scenario. Take a typical industrial site: a main building with a proper sprinkler system, and everything around it — the pump house, the transformer yard, the workshop, the store, the loading bay. The centre is covered. The edges, where most fires actually start, often are not.

Fire load concentrates in the unglamorous parts of a site: switchgear, pump rooms, solvent stores, workshops, and areas where waste accumulates. These are also the areas most likely to be added later, without anyone revisiting the overall protection strategy. The result is a building that is well protected in the part that was designed and unprotected in the part that grew.

Typical impact. A site-wide review of hazard, occupancy and existing coverage — rather than a single-system top-up — closes those gaps and is normally far cheaper than treating them one failure at a time.

  • Failure mode: Outbuildings and yard areas left outside the protection strategy
  • Primary levers: Site-wide hazard review, coverage mapping
  • Also required: Interface between systems and site access
  • Review rhythm: On every change of use or extension
