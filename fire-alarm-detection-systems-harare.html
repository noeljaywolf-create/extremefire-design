Fire detection and alarm

Why most alarm failures are design failures, not equipment failures

The scenario. An addressable panel is installed, a hundred devices are wired in, and the system passes its commissioning test. Six months later a fire starts on an unoccupied floor at 02:00. The alarm sounds — and the question is whether anyone up the road knows where to look.

Detection only protects people when the information it produces is usable. That means the zone a device reports on has to match the way the building is actually used, the sound levels have to be heard over real plant noise, and the cause-and-effect matrix has to describe precisely what the system will do: which notification appliances sound, which doors release, which dampers close, and whether the suppression system is permitted to operate.

Typical impact. Verified zone mapping and a signed cause-and-effect matrix turn an alarm into an instruction. Without them, responders arrive to a panel showing a device number and a floor plan drawn three tenants ago.

  • Failure mode: Alarm sounds without usable location information
  • Primary levers: Zone mapping, cause-and-effect matrix
  • Also required: Interface between alarm and suppression
  • Re-test when: Layout, tenancy or plant changes
