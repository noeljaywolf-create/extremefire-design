Compliance

Compliance is a record, not a certificate

The scenario. A building passes inspection. Twelve months later the insurer, the landlord or a corporate client asks for evidence of ongoing compliance, and the answer is a laminated certificate on a wall that says nothing about the last twelve months.

Most compliance failures are not caused by a missing sprinkler head. They are caused by a gap in the paper trail and in the physical upkeep: a system left impaired without a record, a valve closed and never reopened, extinguishers that passed their date, a fire door wedged open by a trolley. Each of these is individually minor and collectively serious, because each one quietly removes a layer of protection that the design assumed was working.

Typical impact. A maintained system with a documented service history converts compliance from an annual scramble into a continuous state — and it is what an auditor, insurer or tenant will actually ask to see.

  • Failure mode: Protection quietly removed without a record
  • Primary levers: Impairment logging, scheduled service, door checks
  • Also required: Signed test certificates per system
  • Review rhythm: Monthly walk-round, annual full inspection

This describes how compliance generally behave across the industry. It is not a record of work carried out for any named client, and the figures shown are indicative rather than as-built.
