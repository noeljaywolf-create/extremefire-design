Automatic sprinkler systems

What a correctly designed sprinkler system changes

The scenario. Consider a single-storey warehouse with 7 m ceilings and palletised stock stored two metres high. The fire load is ordinary, the building is ordinary, and nothing about the fabric suggests a problem. The deciding factor is entirely in the water.

Two sprinklers rarely extinguish a warehouse fire. What they do is cool the ceiling gases and the steel structure so the fire cannot reach flashover, buying the time an occupant or a responding crew needs. Whether that works comes down to commodity classification, sprinkler spacing and temperature rating — and to whether the hydraulic calculation still holds after ten years of racking changes.

Typical impact. A design matched to the actual storage height and commodity keeps ceiling temperatures below the flashover threshold for long enough to matter. A design that assumed lighter goods, or that was never recalculated after the storage layout changed, does not.

  • Failure mode: Flashover before suppression takes effect
  • Primary levers: Commodity class, spacing, temperature rating
  • Water supply: Hydraulic calculation against actual demand
  • Recalculate when: Racking, storage height or commodity changes
